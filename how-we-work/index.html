How it works

Nothing runs on its own on day one. Every agent is tested on your past records, runs alongside your staff first, and needs a person's sign-off for anything that matters.

The first 30 days

From first message to a working agent

Write or talk

You describe the problem. A written reply by the next business day; calls are 20 minutes, early morning or after 5pm.

Fixed proposal

Within three business days: a fixed fee, a timeline and a definition of done.

Build and test

Built in your own systems and replayed on a period of your past records agreed in the proposal. You get a one-page test report: how many it cleared, held and stopped, and every case where it disagreed with what your team actually did, measured against the pass mark agreed in writing.

Shadow mode

The agent runs alongside your team. Its work is compared with theirs before anything goes live.

Hand over

Documentation, the decision log in your own accounts, and a review with you at day 30. The rules, scripts and procedure files are yours to keep, so you aren't tied to Beiz to keep it running.

How it's built

Rules first. AI only for the exceptions.

Most finance work follows rules, so rules do most of the work. That keeps every step traceable and the running costs low.

Rules first

Fixed rules do the routine checking: matching amounts, dates, suppliers and invoice numbers. They behave the same way every time and cost nothing to run.

AI for the exceptions

AI only looks at what the rules can't settle, like a supplier invoice laid out in an unusual way. It hands back a structured answer for the rules to check again.

Figures from PDFs

PDF tables are positions on a page, not a spreadsheet, which is why general AI tools often misread them. Tables are read separately from the text, and every figure keeps a link to its document, page and line before it's checked against your ledger.

A person decides

Anything held, unusual or over your limit goes to a named person, and a hold always wins: nothing further down the line can release it. Every run is kept in a history your staff can read, step by step.

How a bill gets in

Sorted, checked for doubles, then handled one at a time

Sort it first

Each attachment is sorted before anything is read: invoice, receipt, remittance advice or statement. A remittance advice never gets entered as a bill, and tiny images from email signatures are dropped before they cost anything.

Check for doubles

Before a bill is added, it's looked up by supplier, ABN, invoice number and total. A forwarded copy or an "updated" invoice is held instead of entered twice.

One record at a time

Rules handle each bill on its own, so a long export can't be skimmed. Only the bills the rules can't settle go to AI, which hands back a structured answer the rules check again.

Don't trust the last code

Receipt apps often reuse last month's account code even when the invoice has changed. The agent checks the coding against the invoice itself and holds anything that doesn't match.

Control status

A control isn't live until it's signed off

Every control moves through four states, and each change is recorded. An AI answer is a draft until a person approves it.

Planned

Written down: what the control checks, who owns it and what happens when it fails.

Built

Running in your systems, but its decisions don't count yet.

Tested in shadow

Run on your past records against the pass mark, then alongside your staff, with every difference explained.

Signed off

A named person at your business, and Beiz, sign off that it works. Only then does it run live within its limits.

Stop conditions

When the work stops and waits for a person

  • A supplier's bank details change
  • An ABN is missing or doesn't match the supplier's earlier bills
  • An invoice number repeats, or an "updated" invoice arrives for one already paid
  • A bill is over your approval limit, or several bills look split to stay under it
  • The monthly spending cap is reached, or a person presses the off switch

A stop can't be released by anything further down the line. Only the named person can clear it, and their decision is logged.

Decision log

Five things every check records

The log lives in your own accounts. Here is what each entry holds.

Record

The bill: supplier, invoice number, date and amount.

Check

The rule that ran, in plain words.

Finding

What the check saw in the records, with the records it compared.

Status

ok, held for a person, or stopped before payment.

Who decides

The named person who clears it, and what they do next. Their decision is logged with their name and the time.

The record behind every check

Every check an agent runs leaves a record: what it looked at, the rule it used, what it found, and who decided. That record lives in your own accounts, not ours. The free bill check runs entirely in your browser; your file isn't sent anywhere.
