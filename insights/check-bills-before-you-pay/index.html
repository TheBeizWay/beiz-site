Five checks to run on every supplier bill before you pay it

Duplicate bills, changed bank details and quiet price rises cost small businesses real money. Here's how to catch them, by hand or automatically in Xero or MYOB.

By Purav Mehta, Chartered Accountant · 6 October 2026 · 4 minute read

Most money lost on supplier bills isn't lost to anything clever. It's a bill paid twice, a price that crept up and nobody noticed, or a payment sent to bank details that changed last week. Five simple checks catch nearly all of it.

1. Have we paid this already?

Look for the same supplier and the same amount within a few weeks, or the same invoice number twice. Duplicates usually come from a bill arriving by email and again by post, or a reminder being entered as a new bill.

2. Have the bank details changed?

This is the one that matters most. Payment-redirection scams work by sending a convincing email saying a supplier has new bank details. Never pay new bank details on the strength of an email. Hold the payment and phone the supplier on a number you already had, not one from the email.

3. Is it over the approval limit?

Set a dollar limit above which a bill needs a second pair of eyes, even in a two-person business. It costs a minute and stops most mistakes that matter.

4. Has the price moved?

Compare the unit price with the last order from the same supplier. A 10% rise might be fine, but you should decide that, not find out at year end.

5. Is this a new supplier?

First bills from new suppliers deserve a quick check: ABN, contact details and whether anyone actually ordered from them.

Doing it automatically

All five checks are rules, which makes them a good fit for automation. An automated workflow can pull new bills from Xero or MYOB each morning, run the checks, and sort them into ready to pay and held for a person, each held bill with its reason. Nothing is paid automatically: you still approve the payment run, you just stop reading every bill line by line.

You can watch it work on our bill-checking demo, which runs these checks on eight made-up bills in your browser.

What it won't do

It won't catch a fake bill from a real supplier with unchanged details and a normal price; that still needs someone who knows what was ordered. And the rules are only as good as your data: if supplier records are messy, cleaning them up comes first.

If you'd like these checks running on your own bills, tell us about your setup. It's usually a few days' work.

General information only. If you think you've paid a scammer, contact your bank immediately and report it at Scamwatch.
