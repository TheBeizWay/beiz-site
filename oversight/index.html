Oversight

People approve. The organisation owns.

A person clicking approve is not the same as someone being accountable. Beiz builds both layers: a human checkpoint on each risky action, and an organisation that owns the workflow, reviews the pattern and can show its working to a board or an auditor.

Plan my controlsFree Privacy Act check

Two layers

Human in the loop, and organisation in the loop

Layer 1 · Human in the loop

Who approves this action?

Each agent has a job description and limits. Routine, reversible work can run; anything that touches money, customers or decisions about people stops and asks a named person. Every approval, hold and block is logged.

Layer 2 · Organisation in the loop

Who owns the workflow?

Every AI tool has a named owner. Someone reviews approvals and overrides on a schedule, escalation paths are written down, and the decision log is kept so the organisation can answer “what did the AI do last Tuesday, and who checked it?”.

A human approval step only counts if the reviewer understands the action, has time to look, and can say no without penalty. That is a design question for the organisation, not just a button.

Risk tiers

Match the checkpoint to the risk

Too few checkpoints is unsafe. Too many and people stop using the tool. The sprint sets a tier for every AI tool and workflow.

TierExampleDefault control
Read-onlySummarising a report or flagging an odd invoiceAllow; sample-check monthly
DraftsDraft email, draft journal, draft replyAllow; a person sends or posts
Writes internallyCoding a transaction, updating a recordBatch review by a named reviewer
External or financialPaying a bill, sending to a customer, changing bank detailsHold; one-by-one approval with a dollar threshold
Decisions about peopleHiring, pay, credit or eligibilityBlock for AI alone; person decides and records why

The decision record

What a good approval record contains

  • Action: what the agent proposes to do.
  • Reason and evidence: why, with the source records.
  • Exposure: the dollar amount or people affected.
  • Alternatives: what else was considered, and what happens if nobody acts.
  • Approver and time: a named person, not “the team”.
  • Outcome and any later change: if the plan changes after approval, it is approved again.

A chat transcript is not an audit record. This is.

Keeping it honest

The monthly review

The organisation layer is a habit, not a document. In monthly controls operations, the decision log is reviewed for approval patterns, overrides, holds that were waved through, and tools that appeared since last time. Findings go to the named owner, and anything the board should see goes in a short note.

Australian AI guidance recommends practices like these, including registers, accountability and human oversight, but it is voluntary. The Privacy Act automated-decision rules starting 10 December 2026 apply only to organisations the Act covers. Legal wording stays with your lawyer.
