AML/CTF for accounting firms, without a compliance team.

Since 1 July 2026, accounting firms that set up companies and trusts, act as directors or trustees, or help clients buy and sell businesses have AML/CTF obligations. Agents can do the routine checks and paperwork. Your compliance officer keeps every decision.

In brief
  • For accounting firms working out whether the services they provide bring them under AUSTRAC's AML/CTF rules.
  • Agents collect, check and file the routine client checks. Your compliance officer decides every risk rating and every report.
  • Sized for small firms. General information, not legal advice.
In short

What the agents do

Check whether the service is caught, confirm the client and its owners, run the screening, draft the risk rating, file the evidence and set the next monitoring date.

What stays with your people

Every possible match, every high-risk client and the decision to take the work on. Your AML/CTF compliance officer stays accountable; we prepare, check and keep the record.

Not legal advice: your AML/CTF program sets the actual rules. Obligations as described by AUSTRAC.

Is your firm caught?

It depends on the services you provide, not on being an accountant

The regulated services accounting firms most often provide are:

  • Setting up or restructuring companies, trusts and partnerships
  • Acting as a director, company secretary, trustee or partner for a client, or arranging for someone else to
  • Acting as a nominee shareholder, or arranging one
  • Providing a registered office or principal place of business address
  • Helping a client buy, sell or transfer a business, or interests in a company or trust
  • Helping with equity or debt financing
  • Receiving, holding or controlling client money or property as part of those services
  • Helping a client buy, sell or transfer real estate

A practice that only does tax returns, BAS and bookkeeping is probably outside scope. If you provide any of the services above, even occasionally, check them against AUSTRAC's guidance for tranche 2 entities or with your adviser.

What firms must do

Your obligations

Enrol with AUSTRAC

Within 28 days of first providing a regulated service.

An AML/CTF program

A written risk assessment and policies, approved by a senior manager, kept up to date and independently evaluated at least every three years.

A compliance officer

A fit and proper person who runs the program day to day. In a sole practice, that can be the principal.

Customer due diligence

Identify and verify the client, and the people who own or control it, before you provide the service. Then keep monitoring.

Reports

A suspicious matter report within three business days (24 hours for terrorism financing), and a threshold transaction report for physical cash of $10,000 or more.

Records and an annual report

Keep records for at least seven years, and report each year on how you met your obligations.

What we don't do

We don't act as your AML/CTF compliance officer, write the legal wording of your program or decide whether to report. Program wording stays with your adviser or lawyer; we hand them the facts. This page is general information, not legal advice.

Questions firms ask

We only do tax returns, BAS and bookkeeping. Are we caught?

Probably not, on those services alone. It turns on what you do, not on being an accountant. If you provide any of the services listed above, even occasionally, check them with AUSTRAC's guidance or your adviser.

Can an AI agent be our AML/CTF compliance officer?

No. The compliance officer has to be a fit and proper person. The agent prepares the checks; your officer makes the decisions and signs off.

What about clients we had before 1 July 2026?

AUSTRAC's summary says you generally only need to do due diligence on existing clients when a suspicious matter report is due or their risk changes significantly to medium or high.

Do you need access to our ledger or client files?

Not for the sprint. It runs on short interviews and the documents you already have. If you go on to set up an agent, it works inside your own systems and screening service, with access agreed in writing.
