AI governance & Privacy Act readiness

Know what your AI is doing before someone asks.

Your staff are already using AI. Some of your software is already making decisions about customers. From 10 December 2026, if the Privacy Act covers you, your privacy policy has to say so. We find it, map it and put sensible controls around it.

The problem

Most businesses can't answer three simple questions: which AI tools are in use, what data goes into them, and which of them make or shape decisions about people. Free tools get adopted quietly. Booking, lending, HR and pricing software ships with automated decisions built in. Nobody owns it.

That's a privacy risk, a reputational risk and, from December 2026, a disclosure obligation for businesses covered by the Privacy Act. It's also the first thing an investor, insurer or large customer will ask about.

How we do it

  1. Find it. A short survey of your team and a review of your software and subscriptions. The free tools included.
  2. Map it. For each use: what data goes in, what comes out, what it decides, and what could go wrong.
  3. Control it. Practical rules people will actually follow, human sign-off where outcomes matter, and a named owner.
  4. Report it. A one-page view for the board or owner, and plain-English wording describing your automated decisions, for your privacy policy. Your lawyer confirms the final policy where needed.

What you get

  • AI registerEvery tool in use, what data it touches, and its risk rating.
  • Automated-decision mapWhich systems decide or shape outcomes for people.
  • AI use policyTwo pages your team will read, not twenty they won't.
  • ControlsSign-offs, limits and logging where they matter.
  • Privacy policy wordingPlain-English description of your automated decisions.
  • Board or owner paperOne page: where you stand and what to do next.

Frameworks we work to

Responsible AI isn't our invention. We just make it practical.

These are the public standards behind our AI policies, controls and sign-offs. Read them for free; we turn them into a one-page policy, an approved-tools list and a checklist your team actually uses.

  • Guidance for AI AdoptionNational AI Centre, Australia. The government's practical starting point for businesses using AI.
  • Privacy and commercially available AI productsOAIC. What the Privacy Act expects when you put personal information into AI tools.
  • Recommendation on the Ethics of AIUNESCO, adopted by 193 member states in 2021. The first global standard: human oversight, transparency, fairness, privacy.
  • What is responsible AI?IBM. A clear explainer of the core pillars: transparency, fairness, robustness and privacy.
  • ISO/IEC 42001The international management-system standard for AI, for organisations that want to be audited against one.

Links go to the publishers' own sites. We're not affiliated with or endorsed by any of them.
